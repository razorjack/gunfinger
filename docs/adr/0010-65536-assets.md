# 0010: 65,536 assets with 16 frame bits

## Status

Accepted (2026-10-09, session 9). Supersedes the split of the 32-bit
posting in ADR 0005 (17 frame bits, 15 asset bits); the rest of ADR 0005
stands. Takes option E of ADR 0007 at full frame resolution as the first
step past 32,768 assets; ADR 0007 stays open for the next limit.

## Context

On 2026-10-09 the NAS peak store reached 32,905 records, and every
NAS-scale command stopped with "the index holds at most 32768 assets"
(experiment 0062). A posting packs the anchor frame and the asset into 32
bits (ADR 0005). 17 frame bits addressed 35 minutes at 16 ms per frame,
only so that the 20-minute default track limit of the brief (§6.1,
[DEFAULT]) fitted. The longest record is 9.6 minutes in the corpus and
14:56.8 in the NAS store, whose configuration stops at 15:00.

ADR 0007's options for going past 32,768 assets: shards by asset range
(A), separate libraries (B), shards by collection (C), wider postings (D:
5 bytes cost 25% more memory, 8 bytes double it), narrower frames (E).
Coarser frames, 32 ms, cost 14% of the sweep margin (experiment 0004).
Fewer frames at 16 ms cost nothing in fidelity, only the longest tracks.

## Decision

- 16 frame bits and 16 asset bits (`index.rs` `FRAME_BITS`): 65,536
  frames of 16 ms address 17:28.6, and 65,536 assets. A posting stays 4
  bytes at full resolution, so bytes per posting and detections do not
  change.
- The default track limit (`max_track`, `--max-track`) is 17:00, 28 s
  below what the frames address, against the brief's 20 minutes. The
  margin keeps the default clear of the limit, as a file's declared
  length decides before decoding and the decoded length near the limit.
- A record whose last peak (peaks are in frame order) lies at or past
  frame 65,536 stops the index build with `IndexError::TooLong`, which
  names the file and the 17:28 it exceeds and says to lower
  `--max-track`. A frame therefore never reaches the asset bits.
  `--max-track` above 17:28 is accepted: `index` stores peak records of
  any length, and only the in-memory index is limited. `doctor` already
  marks a longest track beyond 17:28 as a problem.
- The next limit: once a library passes 65,536 assets, shard the index
  by asset range (ADR 0007, option A), each shard with this layout.

## Consequences

- Tracks between 17:00 and 20:00 are no longer indexed by default. The
  corpus (longest 9.6 minutes) and the NAS configuration (15:00) do not
  change. A store indexed under a longer limit keeps its records; the
  default leaves them out of searches, as any narrower range does, and
  their skip notes stay valid (a note holds while the limit in effect is
  not above the one it records), so files over 20:00 are not decoded
  again.
- Detection at 262 tracks is identical (`regress session-9-start`,
  experiment 0062).
- `doctor` counts assets against 65,536 and points here.
- The `u32` positions of the offsets table address 4.29 billion
  postings. At 74.8 postings per second (experiment 0035), 65,536 assets
  reach that only at a mean length of 14.6 minutes; the NAS mean is 6.26
  minutes (1.84 billion postings, 7.4 GB in memory at 65,536 assets).
  Memory, not addressing, is the practical limit before 65,536 assets.
- The on-disk layout recommended in ADR 0005 codes frames and asset gaps
  as varints, so it does not depend on this split.
