# 0062: past 32,768 assets with 16 frame bits

**Question.** Items 1 and 2 of session 9: the NAS store holds 32,905
records. Where does the 32,768-asset limit stop a command, and does
moving one bit from the frame to the asset (ADR 0010) leave detection
unchanged?

**Item 1, the limit.** `gunfinger stats --config
~/.config/gunfinger/nas-dnb.toml` with the start build (`9fda7db`):
"Error: × the index holds at most 32768 assets", exit 1.

| Run | Wall | User | System | Peak footprint |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| With the library (NAS listed over Ethernet) | 57.0 s | 22.9 s | 4.5 s | 20 MB |
| `--store-only` | 24.5 s | 22.6 s | 1.8 s | 28 MB |

The error comes from the counting pass, after 32,768 records have been
read and hashed; the difference, about 32 s, is the NAS listing.

**Change.** `index.rs`: `FRAME_BITS` 17 -> 16, so `MAX_FRAMES` is 65,536
(17:28.6 at 16 ms) and `MAX_ASSETS` 65,536. Default track limit 20:00 ->
17:00. `TooLong` names the file and 17:28 and says to set `--max-track`
to 17:28 or less; `TooManyAssets` says how to leave files out; `doctor`
counts against 65,536 and points to ADR 0010. Tests: the round trip at
asset and frame 65,535; 65,536 assets counted and the next refused; a
record ending at frame 65,535 keeps its frame; a peak at frame 65,536
refused with the 17:28 message.

**Commands.** `scripts/check.sh` (green: 102 core, 48 harness, 34 CLI,
13 + 12 end-to-end tests); `gunfinger-eval regress session-9-start`
(`work/bin/s9-frames16/`, 270 s wall, 2,209 s user, 0.38 GB).

**Result.** Identical: 720 of 720 sweep queries, and all 87, 93 and 79
detections of the development scan and its two leave-outs (11/11, 8/11,
0/11, 0 wrong). The calibrate line is session 8's (weakest identifying
680 -> 658; false candidates at 30 hits 18 -> 46): it compares the
baseline with every report in `work/reports/`. Postings stay 4 bytes.

**Conclusions.** A 4-byte posting addresses 65,536 assets at full frame
resolution, at the price of a 17-minute default track limit.
