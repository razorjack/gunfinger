# 0298: a set-aside threshold by posting count, after two prunes and under growth

**Question** (brief 88, registered in `df1ce0e`). Does a fixed number of postings per list, replacing ADR 0014's fullest 1%, stop a library change from moving detections of untouched files?
**Method.** Variant V: lists above T = 9,932 postings are set aside (T derived once from today's whole NAS index, the longest list today's cut keeps; 10,865 of 1,086,456 lists; `work/indexes/library-nas-dnb-peaks.set-aside.json`, a sidecar, so ADR 0011's file format is unchanged). Today's rule R: the fullest 1% of each index searched. The prunes of sessions 64 (4 files) and 75 (8) are re-created in memory by `--replay-index` from the saved index before session 64's prune less the pruned files (`--replay-leave-out`); less the 12 it has today's lists, less the 4 those before session 75's prune (digests). `regress`-as-run per arm (sweep 720 queries; Stakka and its leave-outs of 3 and 11; 2003 and Nocny scans), 16 workers. `work/s88/queue.sh`, `compare.sh`, `refits.py`; data `data/0298-set-aside-threshold.json`.

| | Today's rule R | V (T = 9,932) |
|---|--:|--:|
| Prune of session 64: sweep queries identical, pruned files' own candidates set aside (of 720) | 713 | 707 |
| Prune of session 75: the same | 695 | 720 |
| Whole-index lists crossing the cut, prune 64; prune 75 | 2; 2 | 3; 3 |
| Lists set aside: before prune 64; after it; after prune 75 | 10,865; 10,863; 10,865 | 10,871; 10,868; 10,865 |
| Confident answer changed, of any file, in any arm | 0 | 0 |
| Largest change of a confident detection's hits | +14 of 303 (Dominion, prune 75) | -1 of 1,178 (Clockwork, prune 64) |

Replay fidelity: R after both prunes equals `session-78-linux-nas` in all 720 queries and every scan; 713 and 695 are 0254's 707 + 6 and 0278's 689 + 6 pruned files' own changes. `off-262` and `off-nas` with the new options unset: `regress --strict` identical.
- **Scans (Stakka, leave-outs 3 and 11, 2003, Nocny; pruned files set aside).** Neither rule changes a confident file or a confident detection's hits. Confident detections refit, in all five scans: R 4 and 4 (speeds by up to 6.5e-6 points); V 4 and 11 (up to 3.5e-5 points); with 1-3 weak detections moved in some scans under each rule. V moved more confident fits on the scans than R, in the same small way.
- **Why V does not stop it.** T is fixed, but a list holding a pruned file's postings shortens and can cross it (9,933 to 9,926, 9,936 to 9,914, ...; 3 lists per prune in the whole index). In the whole index prune 64 did not move R's cut (9,938 before and after: its 2 lists shortened across it, as under V); prune 75 did (9,938 to 9,932, both of R's lists crossing without shortening), which V removes. Over the two prunes V changed 13 of 1,440 sweep comparisons against 32 (bar 2), but in the first prune 13 against 7.
- **At the same index (D, one-time).** Whole-index scans identical under V and R. The sweep (720) and leave-outs carry their own cuts under R, so V differs: 534 queries identical, 186 changed (138 refits, 48 weak); leave-out 11 one weak more; no confident answer changed; 210 of the sweep's 1,363 confident detections moved, 11 by hits (at most 1.17%).
- **Growth (exact copies of drawn records, sweep only; copies' own detections set aside; 5%: 2,034 copies, 1,198.1M postings; 20%: 8,138, 1,369.1M).** Against the rule's own ungrown arm: R moves 323 (5%) and 412 (20%) of 720 queries, largest confident hits change 4.4% and 17.9%; V with T kept 648 (5%) and 684 (20%), 36% and 36% (Siren Bass at -3%, 248 to 337 hits, confident in both; a low-hit detection); V with T re-derived at 20% (T = 11,904, 1.000% set aside) 399, 17.9%. V with T kept sets aside 1.082% at 5% and 1.350% at 20% (14,668 lists). V against R at the same grown index: 642 and 684 queries, 178 with T re-derived; no confident file changes in any comparison (bar 4). So at 5% a kept T moves about twice as many queries as R; re-deriving costs nothing against R. Copies add postings, no hashes, so real growth is not shown.
- **262 tracks** (T = 118, 6,029 of 605,333 lists; 12 drawn corpus files, in no development set's manifest and no sweep panel, 4.5% of the library): of 720 sweep queries, identical after the prune R 229, V 191; no confident file changes under either; at the same 265-file index V and R differ in 574 of 720 (one-time, whole-index scans identical). A prune of that share moves most fits under either rule.

- **Tracing (bar 2).** `sweep --set-aside-like` under the threshold (`work/s88/trace.sh`): state B's postings searched with the lists T sets aside in state A (both the sweep's index, held-out files left out) gives A's sweep less the pruned files' own detections in all 720 queries, so V's prune-64 moves are the lists that lost pruned postings and crossed T (2 lists in the sweep's index: 9,933 to 9,930 and 9,931, 3 and 2 postings of the pruned files). Prune 75 moved no sweep query under V. For the scans, which have no `--set-aside-like`, the same cause is an inference.
- **Limits.** T was derived from today's index, after both prunes, as registered, so prune 75 tests a threshold chosen after it (B's and A's own cut, 9,938, would differ); two prunes and one placement of T, on one store.

**Conclusion.** All four registered conditions hold: same lists at today's index (test and `at-D` whole-index scans); 13 against 32 of 1,440 sweep comparisons changed over two prunes (half is 16), no confident change, V's changes (12 refits, 1 weak) of kinds R shows, and traced as above; the one-time difference from R changes no confident answer; at 5% growth 1.082% set aside, at 20% re-derived, no confident answer differs. But V does not make a prune move nothing: one prune of the two moved more sweep queries (13 against 7), the scans' confident fits moved more (15 against 8), lists crossing T by their own length stay, and at 5% growth a kept T moves about twice as many queries as today's rule. The gain is smaller than the idea promised. ADR 0014's rule stays the default. An ADR would need the threshold in the saved index's header and a growth rule; decisions queue item 11.
